Good images can fool anyone, and generators keep getting better. These clues help, but none of them is definitive. Use them together, and always check the context.

Clues inside the image

  • Hands and fingers. Count them. Extra, missing or merged fingers, or joints that bend the wrong way, are a warning sign.
  • Text and signs. Letters that look almost right but don't form real words, or that change from one line to the next.
  • Light and shadows. Shadows that point in different directions, reflections that don't match the scene, or light that comes from nowhere.
  • Backgrounds. Edges that blur or melt into the subject, objects that merge together, or patterns that repeat in strange ways.
  • Symmetry and repeated details. Earrings, buttons or windows that don't match, or the same detail copied over and over.
  • Skin and textures. Skin that looks too smooth, or fabric and hair that look painted.

Clues outside the image

  • Origin. Find where the image first appeared. Who published it, and does that source say who made it?
  • Reverse image search. A reverse image search tool can find older copies, with their captions and dates.
  • Content credentials. Some images carry content credentials (C2PA) that say how they were made. When they're there, read them. When they're missing, that proves nothing.
  • Invisible watermarks. Some AI tools add invisible watermarks, such as SynthID. You can't see them, and a missing watermark doesn't prove that an image is real.

What these clues can't do

Real photos can look strange too, with heavy editing, night shots, poor cameras or heavy compression. And a clean-looking image can still be AI. That's why you should check the source before you decide.

No clue is definitive. Generators keep improving, so a trick that works today may stop working.

Practice
